    Our Heritage

    The documented record of this Lodge, drawn from the Grand Lodge of the Philippines.

    The Lodge

    Noli Me Tangere Lodge takes its name from the novel that José Rizal published in 1887 — a book that named what colonial Philippines would not say aloud, and that cost its author his life nine years later. The Lodge was organised in Pasay in 1917 by thirteen Masons, and constituted the following March by Manuel L. Quezon, then Grand Master, who would later become the first President of the Philippine Commonwealth.

    What follows is the Lodge's documented history, including the parts that are not a straight line. It went bankrupt in the 1930s, lost its charter, failed twice to revive, and was eventually re-chartered under a different number. That record is set out below as the Grand Lodge sets it out, rather than smoothed into a continuous institutional story.

    The dates and names on this page come from two sources: the Lodge's own charter of 1918, reproduced below, and the Lodge's entry in the Grand Lodge of the Philippines directory, linked at the foot of this page. Anything neither source records is marked as unresolved rather than filled in.

    Timeline

    1. 1917

      Dispensation

      On 13 August — A.L. 5917, as the charter records it — Grand Master William H. Taylor issues a dispensation to thirteen Masons to assemble as a Lodge in the Town of Pasay, Province of Rizal. The first meeting is held the same day.

    2. 1918

      Chartered and constituted

      The Grand Lodge grants the charter at its annual communication in January. The instrument itself is executed at the City of Manila on 18 March 1918 — Anno Lucis 5918 — signed by Grand Master Manuel L. Quezon and attested by Grand Secretary Newton C. Comfort, PGM. It names the Lodge, in full, Noli Me Tangere Lodge No. 42, F. & A.M.

      The same day, Quezon constitutes the Lodge at the Blue Lodge Hall of the Masonic Temple on the Escolta. The charter appoints the first line:

      • Manuel de Santos Worshipful Master
      • Adam T. Tanner Senior Warden
      • Dionisio San Agustin Junior Warden
    3. 1920s

      Growth

      Membership rises toward two hundred. Dionisio San Agustin, named Junior Warden on the charter, goes on to serve as Master in 1921, 1922, 1927 and 1928.

    4. 1931–33

      Decline

      The Depression reaches the Lodge. Membership falls to 88 in 1931 and 53 by 1933, the year Primitivo Lovina serves as Master.

    5. 1934

      Bankruptcy and arrest of the charter

      The Lodge goes bankrupt. La Urbana, Inc. forecloses on its temple and land at Libertad Street, Pasay. In the last months of the year Grand Master Manuel Camus arrests the charter. On 3 September Camus had already rejected a petition — signed by nearly the entire membership — for a dispensation to form a new Pasay lodge.

    6. 1946

      First attempt to revive

      Twenty-five former members petition Grand Master Michael Goldenberg. He rejects the request on 9 March.

    7. 1947–48

      Second attempt, surrendered

      Grand Master Emilio P. Virata issues a dispensation on 7 March 1947, naming Abundio C. del Rosario Master, Roman Amor Senior Warden and Modesto Cenido Junior Warden. On 11 March 1948 the members resolve to surrender it; del Rosario delivers the dispensation to the Grand Secretary on 3 June.

    8. 1955

      Reinstated as No. 42

      On 26 December, Grand Master Camilo Osias approves a petition from sixteen Master Masons, and the Lodge is reinstated as No. 42.

    9. 1957

      Dissolved and re-chartered as No. 148

      In April, a committee led by WB Juan Nabong, Sr. reports that the 1955 revival lacked legal support. The Grand Lodge approves its recommendation: the Lodge is treated as a new lodge under dispensation dating from 26 December 1955, the former No. 42 is dissolved, and the Lodge is chartered as No. 148.

    10. 1990

      Renumbering decreed, then rejected

      Grand Master Juan Nabong, Jr. decrees that No. 148 be renumbered No. 42. The Cable Tow announces the change — but at the April 1990 Annual Communication the Grand Lodge rejects it.

    On the number

    The charter above is the Lodge's founding instrument, and it reads No. 42 in Quezon's own grant. It settles what the Lodge was chartered as in 1918. It does not, on its own, settle what the Lodge is numbered now — that question turns on what came after it.

    The Grand Lodge directory lists this Lodge as No. 42 today. The narrative on that same page records that the charter above was arrested in 1934, that the 1957 proceedings treated the Lodge as newly constituted and dissolved the former No. 42, chartering it as No. 148, and that the 1990 decree restoring No. 42 was rejected at the Annual Communication that April.

    How the Lodge came to carry No. 42 again after that is not explained in any source we have found. A separate Grand Lodge page refers to No. 148 having regained “its old number, namely, 42”, without giving a date or an authority.

    We set this out rather than choose a version. Brethren who want the full proceedings should consult the Grand Secretary; corrections from the Grand Lodge are welcome and will be reflected here.
